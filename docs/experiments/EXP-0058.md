# EXP-0058 — parallel activation-tile packing in the MoE GEMM: +6% on the bucket

Status: PASSED
Date: 2026-11-27

## Problem

`gemm_q4_K_q8_K_mt` runs 1852 times per 1024-token chunk (38 MoE layers x 64
expert buckets x 2 calls for gate and up). Each call packs the activation tiles
serially, then fans out. Fitting `t = a + b*n` to two shapes gives the fixed
part:

| m | t(n=1536) | t(n=11776) | fixed b | share of the 1536 call |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 34 | 0.089 ms | 0.711 | — | (fit unstable, only 2 points) |
| 128 | 0.205 | 1.158 | 0.063 | **30.5%** |
| 512 | 0.700 | 3.949 | 0.213 | **30.4%** |
| 1024 | 1.439 | 8.172 | 0.429 | **29.8%** |

A stable 30% of the bucket call was outside the GEMM itself. Isolated
`pack_a_tiles` at m=512 is 0.098 ms against a 0.70 ms call — the rest of the gap
is the pool wake-up.

## Fix

`pack_a_tiles` gained a tile-range overload (`jt0`, `jt1`); tiles are independent
so no locking is needed. `gemm_q4_K_q8_K_mt` now allocates the tile vector, packs
it across the pool, and then runs the GEMM in the same worker loop body — two
`pool.run` calls per invocation would have cost more than the pack, see below.

## Measurement

n=1536, k=2048, 16 threads, median of 30-200 reps, alternating order:

| m | before | after | |
|---|---|---|---|
| 34 | 0.089 ms (1210 GMAC/s) | **0.0792 ms (1351)** | +12% |
| 512 | 0.717 ms (2246) | **0.679 ms (2372)** | +5.6% |
| 1024 | 1.464 ms (2201) | **1.347 ms (2392)** | +8.7% |
| n=11776, m=512 | 3199 GMAC/s | 3190 | unchanged, as expected |

End to end, 16 threads, CPU only:

| config | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| prefill 16384 tok | 399.1 tok/s | **406.5 / 417.3** |
| prefill 522 tok | 493.1 tok/s | 492.5 / 488.9 |

Golden points unchanged: `argmax=440 logit=16.12790` (522 tok), `argmax=17778
logit=28.45286` (16384 tok). 105/105 tests pass.

## A wrong turn recorded

A first version did the pack in a separate `pool.run` before the GEMM's own
`pool.run`. It made m=512 faster (0.700 -> 0.676) but **m=34 worse, 0.089 ->
0.144**, because at m=34 the pack is only two tiles and a second fan-out costs
more than it saves. The measured all-shapes table is what caught it; the
m=512-only table would have shipped a regression. Merging the two phases back
into one worker loop is what makes it a win at every m.

Two earlier measurements in this investigation were artefacts and are recorded so
they are not repeated:

1. A `scal.cpp` sweep in increasing thread count reported **n=1536 at 16 threads
   as 569 GMAC/s vs 3090 at n=11776** — a 0.18x ratio. Cause: the previous
   `ThreadPool`'s workers were still inside their spin budget while the next
   pool's binary ran, so two pools competed for the same 16 cores. With a 150 ms
   sleep between arms the same sweep gives 2075 vs 3084 = 0.67x, which matches
   the engine. **Never construct competing thread pools in one process.**
2. Fitting `t = a + b*n` from only two points at m=34 gave a negative intercept
   (-0.004 ms). Two points fit a line, they do not validate it.

## Remaining fixed cost

After the fix, at m=512 the fixed part is still 27% and at m=1024 39% (that
figure is dominated by noise in the two-point fit, but the m=34 case is now the
one to watch: 0.0792 ms measured against 0.032 ms of ideal 1/16 compute means
~60% is still wake-up, and the pool's `run` costs 14.3 us empty at 16 threads).
The next lever there is not arithmetic: it is calling the pool once per layer
instead of once per bucket, which needs the weight bands of all 64 experts
unpacked in one pass.
