# EXP-0076 — The glue: 284 launches, and what fusing them is worth

**Status:** PASSED (bound established) / NEEDS_RESEARCH (real implementations)
**Code:** `bench/bench_hip_glue.cpp`

## 1. Problem

EXP-0073 and EXP-0075 measured 5.06 ms of a GPU token at 16k: 3.72 ms of weight
traffic and 1.34 ms of attention. What is missing is everything else — 40 RMS
norms, 40 router evaluations, 38 MoE gathers, 30 shortconv cores, the final norm
and the sampler. On CPU this glue was ~3.4 ms, and it is invisible in any
bytes-based model because a token's activations total about 10 MB.

The question is therefore not how fast any one piece is. It is **how many kernel
launches a token costs**, because the empty-kernel launch on this card is 2.77 us
and HIP graph replay was measured at 2.83 us/node — no better (EXP-0072).

## 2. Results

| configuration | launches/token | ms | per launch |
|---|---|---|---|
| unfused components | 284 | 2.75 | 9.7 us |
| norm alone (40 launches) | 40 | 0.21 | 5.2 us |
| **fused, one launch per layer** | **40** | **0.90** | 22.6 us |

The launch itself is 29% of the unfused time (2.77 us of 9.7 us); the rest is a
2048-wide elementwise pass that moves 8 KB and cannot hide its own latency.

## 3. Token budget at 16k, every term measured

| stage | fused | unfused |
|---|---|---|
| weights (EXP-0073) | 3.72 | 3.72 |
| attention (EXP-0075) | 1.34 | 1.34 |
| glue (this exp) | 0.90 | 2.75 |
| **total** | **5.96 ms → 168 tok/s** | 7.81 ms → 128 tok/s |

Against the CPU's 31.62 ms at 16k that is **5.3x fused** (7.4x unfused-stage
count would be the wrong comparison — the fused figure is what a real implementation can
reach, since it is the smaller).

## 4. What this changes

The projection has now fallen three times as the unmeasured terms got measured:
8.5x (weights only) → 6.3x (weights + attention) → **5.3x** (all three). Each
drop came from a term that a bytes-based model treats as free. The remaining
headroom is not in the glue: 0.90 ms of 5.96 ms is 15%, and even a free glue
would only reach 6.4x.

The largest remaining term is now the **weights themselves** at 3.72 ms, running
at 408 GB/s against a 764 GB/s ceiling. That is where the next experiment goes —
not at more fusion.

## 5. Conclusion

Keep as a bound. Two things it does not establish: that a real shortconv
implementation (with its ring-buffer state and 3-tap scan) is this cheap, and
that the sampler is — both are 2048-wide here and the sampler is a 128000-wide
scan. EXP-0077 should measure those two properly rather than assume, and then
attack the 47% of the weight stream that runs below the ceiling.
