# EXP-0070 — q3_K on the MoE gate/up experts: the PPL anomaly is corpus-specific

**Status:** PASSED (measurement) / REJECTED (as a usable win)
**Date:** 2026-11-27
**Follows:** EXP-0051, which recorded `gu → q3_K` as `−5.24% PPL` and marked it
`NEEDS_RESEARCH` because a 3-bit format beating 4-bit is not physically plausible.

## Hypothesis

EXP-0051 left two live explanations: (a) a measurement artefact of
`--allow-requantize`, or (b) a real mechanism, most plausibly that q3_K uses
**16-element sub-blocks** while q4_K/q5_K use **32-element** ones, so a 3-bit
format could genuinely quantise a heavy-tailed weight distribution better.

## Measurements

Repeated on the same 24-chunk WikiText-2 protocol as EXP-0051, plus controls:

| model | 8 chunks | 24 chunks | vs base |
|---|---|---|---|
| base Q4_K_M | 11.8316 | 12.8833 | — |
| gate+up → q3_K | 11.3461 | 12.2993 | **−4.5%** |
| gate+up → q5_K | 11.9049 | — | +0.6% |
| gate+up → q2_K | 12.7004 | — | +7.3% |
| gate only → q3_K | 11.5909 | — | −2.0% |
| up only → q3_K | 11.3054 | — | −4.4% |

Reproducible and thread-independent: base PPL is bit-identical at `-t 1`, `-t 8`
and `-t 16` (11.8316 every time).

**Mechanism refuted by direct measurement.** `/tmp/gran.cpp` quantises weights
drawn from the measured distribution (Laplace, σ = 0.0118, max 0.118, matching
`/tmp/wstat.cpp` on the real tensors: rms 0.0118, mean|w| 0.0095) and reconstructs
them with ggml's own `from_float`/`to_float`:

| format | bytes/weight | relative reconstruction error |
|---|---|---|
| q2_K (16 × 2 bit) | 0.3281 | 0.3441 |
| q3_K (16 × 3 bit) | 0.4297 | 0.1762 |
| q4_K (32 × 4 bit) | 0.5625 | 0.0846 |
| q5_K (32 × 5 bit) | 0.6875 | 0.0428 |

q3_K's reconstruction error is **2.08x worse** than q4_K's, and q2_K, which shares
q3_K's 16-element granularity, is far worse still. So finer sub-blocks are not the
mechanism — the anomaly is not accuracy.

Weight-space deviation on the real files (`/tmp/qerr.cpp`, dequantising both
GGUFs): rel **0.1508** for the gate/up tensors. A 15% weight deviation is what
"improves" perplexity here.

**Second corpus, opposite direction.** Same protocol on a Rust-source corpus
(`/home/medovarov/kata-work/corpus/code.raw`, 268 KB of the project's own sources,
8 chunks):

| model | PPL |
|---|---|
| base | 3.4474 |
| gate+up → q3_K | **3.5534 (+3.1%, worse)** |

## Conclusion

The effect does not transfer: −4.5% on WikiText-2, +3.1% on source code. A change
that is freely positive on one corpus and negative on another at the same bit
budget is a per-corpus accident of quantisation noise, **not a win**, and must not
be shipped. Nothing is adopted.

What remains true and useful: the pre-existing verdict from EXP-0051 stands for
`ffn_down_exps` (+6.20% PPL), and `gate/up → q3_K` must now be recorded as
**corpus-dependent, do not adopt**, rather than `NEEDS_RESEARCH`. Any future
quantisation claim needs at least two corpora before it is quoted — one corpus
cannot distinguish a mechanism from noise.

## Reproduction

```
llama-quantize --allow-requantize --tensor-type "ffn_gate_exps=q3_K" \
    --tensor-type "ffn_up_exps=q3_K" <base.gguf> <out.gguf> q4_K_M 16
HIP_VISIBLE_DEVICES="" ROCR_VISIBLE_DEVICES="" llama-perplexity \
    -m <out.gguf> -f <corpus> -t 16 -c 2048 --chunks 24
```

Note the positional `q4_K_M` argument is mandatory (`llama_quantize: missing ftype`
otherwise), and `--chunks 1` is never a valid sample.
