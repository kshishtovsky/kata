# EXP-0080 — does the hybrid projection survive the layer dependency?

**Status:** PASSED
**Date:** 2026-11-27

## Problem

EXP-0078 priced expert residency from real routing and reported 82 tok/s at a
2 GB budget (2.5x over CPU-only). But it computed that as
`max(sum(CPU bytes), sum(GPU bytes)) / rate` — i.e. it assumed a token's CPU
share and GPU share run concurrently and the token waits for the slower one
**once**.

That is not how a transformer runs. Layer *i*'s router needs layer *i-1*'s
output, and layer *i-1* finishes only when the slowest device holding any of its
experts finishes. So a token costs `sum over layers of max(CPU_i, GPU_i)`, and
that sum is always ≥ the free-running figure. The gap is the price of the
dependency, and it decides whether a hybrid is worth building at all.

## Method

`tools/kata_place` now builds both columns from the same captured routing:

- **today** — every expert byte on the CPU at 68.3 GB/s (the rate a real decode
  achieves, EXP-0069; using the 73.5 peak makes every plan look better than the
  machine can deliver).
- **ideal** — dependency ignored: `max(total CPU, total GPU)`.
- **chain** — `sum over layers of max(CPU_i, GPU_i)`, the physical floor.
- **loss** — `chain / ideal`.

Fixed per-token bytes are now read from the GGUF (all non-`_exps.` tensors) and
the KV term is computed from the run's own context length
(`2 * n_head_kv * head_dim * 4 * n_attn_layer` per position), not hard-coded at
16k.

## Results — 24B, 4128-token prefix, 280 GB/s assumed

| VRAM | experts | today | ideal | chain | loss |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 | 26.0 | 26.0 | 26.0 | 1.00x |
| 1 GB | 25 | 25.8 | 13.5 | 13.7 | 1.01x |
| 2 GB | 189 | 25.2 | 12.8 | 13.0 | 1.01x |
| 4 GB | 515 | 23.7 | 11.3 | 11.5 | 1.02x |
| 6 GB | 842 | 21.9 | 9.5 | 10.0 | 1.05x |
| 8 GB | 1169 | 20.1 | 7.7 | 8.6 | **1.11x** |
| 12 GB | 1823 | 16.4 | 4.1 | 7.0 | 1.72x |
| 16 GB | 1979 | 15.4 | 3.2 | 6.8 | 2.11x |

At 400 GB/s the shape is unchanged and the loss slightly smaller
(8 GB: 8.1 ms / 1.05x; 16 GB: 5.4 ms / 1.72x).

## Conclusion

**The projection survives where it matters.** At a 2-8 GB budget the dependency
costs 1-11%, because CPU and GPU carry comparable shares per layer and a
balanced split keeps `max(CPU_i, GPU_i)` near its average. Loss only becomes
material at 12 GB+ (1.7-2.1x), where the CPU share is small but every layer
still waits for it.

So the honest numbers are:

- 2 GB → 13.0 ms/token = **77 tok/s**, 1.9x over today's 26.0 ms
- 8 GB → 8.6 ms/token = **116 tok/s**, 2.3x
- 16 GB → 6.8 ms/token = 147 tok/s, 3.8x (and this is where the dependency
  starts eating the win)

The build order follows from this: the win is dominated by getting the **fixed
per-token set** (674 MB weights + KV) off the CPU bus, which needs no expert
placement at all. Expert placement is the second-order term and can be
approximate.

## Caveats, stated rather than buried

- Fixed bytes are split **evenly over layers**. `token_embd.weight` is 215 MB
  of the 674 MB and is read once at the lm_head, not spread across 40 layers, so
  the chain column slightly flatters the deep case. It does not change the
  conclusion at 2-8 GB, where the expert term dominates.
- `gpu_gbs` is a parameter, not a measurement on a consumer card. This machine
  measured 764 GB/s read-only (EXP-0071); 280 and 400 are the realistic
  consumer range.
- Nothing here overlaps compute with transfer. A real implementation also has to
  pay one synchronisation per MoE layer, ~0.5 ms/token at 38 layers — small
  against 8.6 ms, but it is the next thing to measure.
